BMO · JLL MTS

Same Goal. A Lot Less Waiting.

The original WORQ procedure and the new one protect the same thing — the FM's authority over cost and funding. What changed is how much has to stop and wait to get there.

Before
Now
Every request, no exceptions Every single request — even a $40 part — had to be reviewed with the FM before anything could happen. "All WORQ requests must be reviewed with the Facility Manager prior to authorization or execution."
A clear line: $499 Routine, small jobs go through right away. FM attention is spent only on the requests that actually call for a decision.
A live conversation, every time Costs had to be validated with the FM directly — a call or meeting — for every job, before work could be scheduled. "Labor and Material costs shall be validated with the FM to assess available OPEX funding."
One form, filled in once The tech enters cost, condition, and what's needed in a simple guided form. It's all there for the FM to review on their own time — nothing to repeat over the phone.
Stop and wait Work could not proceed until funding, classification, and cost were all separately confirmed by the FM. "DO NOT PROCEED — await FM Validation, OPEX Funding Review, and Unplanned Capital Tracking Determination."
Only the hard cases wait The easy cases aren't held up by the same process built for the hard ones. A real judgment call — a vendor, a capital decision — is flagged clearly and still goes to the FM, without freezing everything else.
A description, and nothing else Decisions were made from a written or verbal account of the problem — no way for the FM to actually see it.
Photos, captioned by the tech The FM sees the actual problem, not just a description of it. A picture closes a gap words can't.
Re-entered by hand After validating everything, the FM (or FMS) still had to create the work order in Corrigo from scratch.
One click, for the clear cases For a plain internal repair, approving the request can also create the Corrigo work order — nothing gets retyped.

What doesn't change

  • The FM still has final say on funding, vendor choice, and whether something is repair, unplanned capital, or future capital.
  • Nothing gets spent past the approved amount without the FM's OK — that hard stop stays exactly as strict.
  • Every job still gets tracked and classified before it's closed out — for the budget and for the capital plan.

The accountability the original process was built to protect stays intact. What disappears is the waiting.
